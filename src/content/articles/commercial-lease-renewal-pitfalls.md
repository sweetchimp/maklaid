A commercial lease looks dormant for years, then suddenly decides your business's economics. Renewal season is where unexamined clauses collect their debt. These are the three provisions that most often surprise Nairobi tenants.

## The silent rent escalation

Many leases provide for rent reviews tied to published indices — but some quietly specify a **floor increase of five percent annually regardless of the index**. In low-inflation years tenants pay well above market movement. Every review clause should be modelled across the full term before signing, not discovered at renewal.

## Repair obligations without schedules

"Tenant shall maintain the premises in good repair" sounds harmless until the dilapidations claim arrives. Without a signed schedule of condition at commencement, the tenant inherits the landlord's decade-old roof, lifts and wiring. Photograph everything at entry; attach the schedule to the lease.

## The renewal window trap

Renewal options commonly require written notice **six to twelve months before expiry** — miss it and the option lapses, leaving the landlord free to re-let or reprice. Diarised notice deadlines belong in every tenant's compliance calendar, alongside the statutory protections where they apply.

## What good preparation looks like

Eighteen months before expiry: reread the lease against current market rents, quantify make-good exposure, and open renewal discussions from a position of alternatives. Tenants who begin early consistently secure better terms than those who begin when the landlord raises the subject.
