Families are usually told probate "takes time." Rarely are they told *where* the time actually goes. Understanding the stages turns an anxious wait into a managed process.

## Stage by stage

**Weeks 1–4: Preparation.** Death certificate, original will (if any), inventory of assets and liabilities, and identification documents for all beneficiaries and dependants. Files stall here more than anywhere — usually waiting on banks and land registries for statements and search certificates.

**Months 1–6: Grant application.** The petition is filed at the High Court or magistrates' court, followed by the statutory notice period during which any objector may come forward. Contested estates leave this stage and enter a very different track.

**Months 6–12: Grant and confirmation.** Once the grant issues, the executor collects assets. Confirmation then applies for the distribution scheme itself — which must account for dependants' statutory entitlements before any testamentary wishes take effect.

**Month 12 onward: Distribution.** Transfers, closures and registrations execute the confirmed scheme. Titles in deceased names finally move.

## What actually causes delay

Incomplete beneficiary documentation, undisclosed assets discovered mid-process, family disagreements allowed to harden into objections, and institutions that respond slowly without a lawyer's follow-up.

## The honest advice

Instruct early, disclose everything, and keep the family informed — most contests are preventable with transparency at stage one. Where a dispute does emerge, mediation resolves the majority of estate disagreements without a full trial.
