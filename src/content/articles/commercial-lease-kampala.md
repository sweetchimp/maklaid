Kampala's commercial property market is competitive. Tenants eager to secure space often sign leases without fully understanding the obligations they are taking on. Here are the clauses that most often surprise tenants.

## 1. Repair and maintenance obligations

Many commercial leases impose a full repairing obligation on the tenant — meaning you are responsible for everything from roof leaks to air conditioning. Before signing, understand:

- What is the tenant's obligation vs. the landlord's?
- Is there a schedule of condition (photographing the state at commencement)?
- Are you liable for structural repairs or just internal maintenance?

**Tip:** Always insist on a schedule of condition. Without one, you may be liable for pre-existing defects when you leave.

## 2. Rent review and escalation

Commercial rents in Kampala are typically reviewed every 2–3 years. The lease may specify:

- Fixed escalation (e.g., 10% every 2 years)
- Market rent review (determined by a valuer)
- CPI-linked adjustment

Understand the mechanism before you commit. A poorly negotiated rent review can double your occupancy costs in five years.

## 3. Break clauses

A break clause allows either party to terminate the lease early. But watch for conditions:

- Minimum notice period (typically 6–12 months)
- Conditions that must be met (vacant possession, no arrears, reinstatement)
- Whether the break is mutual or one-sided

**Tip:** A break clause with onerous conditions is not really a break clause at all. Negotiate realistic conditions.

## 4. Use restrictions

The lease may restrict how you use the premises. Common restrictions include:

- Prohibited activities (e.g., no manufacturing, no entertainment)
- Hours of operation
- Signage requirements
- Subletting restrictions

Ensure the permitted use matches your business needs — including any future expansion plans.

## 5. Dilapidations

At the end of the lease, you may be required to:

- Reinstatement to original condition
- Remove all tenant improvements
- Repair any damage beyond fair wear and tear

The landlord's dilapidations claim can be substantial. Protect yourself with:

- A schedule of condition at commencement
- Photographic records throughout the lease
- A dilapidations protocol agreed at the start

## FAQ

**Do I need a lawyer for a commercial lease?**
While not legally required, a lawyer's review can save you thousands. The cost of a lease review is a fraction of the cost of a bad lease.

**How long should a commercial lease be?**
Typically 3–5 years for office space, 5–10 years for retail. Longer leases may offer better terms but reduce flexibility.

**Can I sublet my commercial premises?**
Only if the lease permits it. Most leases require the landlord's consent, which may be withheld. Check the subletting clause before making any arrangements.
