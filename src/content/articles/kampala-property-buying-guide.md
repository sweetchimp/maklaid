Kampala's property market rewards enthusiasm and punishes shortcuts. The gap between a dream purchase and a courtroom dispute is almost always diligence that never happened. Run these checks before any deposit moves.

## The ten essentials

1. **Official registry search** — confirms the registered owner and any encumbrances on the title at the Ministry of Lands, Housing and Urban Development.
2. **Encumbrance tracing** — charges, caveats and restrictions must be discharged, not merely acknowledged.
3. **Survey and registry-index-map verification** — confirms the parcel boundaries match what the seller claims.
4. **Rates and rent clearance** — Kampala Capital City Authority (KCCA) rates and ground rent must be current.
5. **Land board consent** — where applicable, the consent of the relevant land board is required before transfer.
6. **Company search** — if the seller is a corporate entity, confirm authority to sell and check for charges.
7. **Succession confirmation** — for estate sales, confirm the grant of probate or letters of administration is valid.
8. **Physical inspection** — verify occupation, boundary walls, access roads and any unapproved structures.
9. **Stamp duty assessment** — understand the stamp duty payable (currently 1% for plots in Kampala, 1.5% elsewhere).
10. **Transfer filing** — ensure the transfer is lodged at the Ministry of Lands within the statutory period.

## Common pitfalls we see

- **Buying from a "family representative" without a grant** — this is the single most common source of disputes. No one can sell estate property without proper legal authority.
- **Ignoring KCCA rates** — outstanding rates become a lien on the property and can block transfer.
- **Assuming leasehold is freehold** — many Kampala properties are leasehold with 49 or 99-year terms. Ground rent and renewal conditions matter.
- **Skipping the physical inspection** — registry records don't show encroachments, informal settlements or unapproved extensions.

## Timeline

A typical property purchase in Kampala takes 6–12 weeks from instruction to registration. The timeline depends on:

- Speed of search results (usually 2–4 weeks)
- Whether land board consent is required (adds 2–4 weeks)
- Stamp duty assessment and payment (1–2 weeks)
- Registration at the Ministry of Lands (2–4 weeks)

## FAQ

**Can I buy property in Uganda as a foreigner?**
Foreigners can own leasehold interests (up to 99 years) but not freehold land. The Constitution restricts freehold ownership to Ugandan citizens. We advise on the best structure for your situation.

**What is stamp duty and how much is it?**
Stamp duty is a tax on property transfers. In Kampala it is 1% of the property value; outside Kampala it is 1.5%. The buyer typically pays.

**Do I need a lawyer for a property purchase?**
While not legally required, it is strongly advisable. Property law in Uganda is complex, and mistakes can cost more than a lawyer's fee. We protect your investment from start to finish.
