Losing a loved one is devastating. The legal process that follows should not add to the burden. Here is a realistic timeline for probate and succession in Uganda — and how we make it smoother.

## The legal framework

Ugandan succession law is governed by the Succession Act (Cap. 162), the Administration of Estates Act, and customary law depending on the deceased's background. The process differs based on whether the deceased left a valid will (testate) or died without one (intestate).

## Timeline overview

### Week 1–2: Immediate steps

- Obtain the death certificate from the relevant hospital or local council
- Secure the deceased's property — change locks, notify banks, cancel utilities
- Locate the will (if one exists) and contact a lawyer

### Week 2–6: Application for grant

- **Testate (with will):** Apply for probate of the will at the High Court
- **Intestate (without will):** Apply for letters of administration
- Both require: death certificate, affidavit of next-of-kin, inventory of estate assets, advertisements in the Uganda Gazette and a local newspaper

### Week 6–12: Court processing

- The court processes the application (typically 4–8 weeks)
- Objections may be filed by interested parties
- Once approved, the executor/administrator takes an oath

### Week 12–24: Estate administration

- Collect and value all estate assets
- Pay debts, taxes and funeral expenses
- Distribute remaining assets according to the will or intestacy rules
- File estate accounts with the court

### Total timeline: 6–12 months

A straightforward estate with cooperative heirs and no disputes typically resolves in 6–12 months. Contested estates can take 2–5 years.

## Common complications

- **Multiple marriages** — Uganda's plural marriage laws create complex priority questions
- **Customary vs. statutory succession** — different rules apply depending on the deceased's background
- **Property in multiple districts** — may require applications in different courts
- **Disputed wills** — challenges to validity based on capacity, undue influence or formal defects

## FAQ

**Can I administer an estate without a lawyer?**
While not legally required, the process is complex and errors can be costly. A lawyer ensures compliance with all legal requirements and protects the administrator from personal liability.

**What if there is no will?**
The estate is distributed according to the Intestate Succession Act. The surviving spouse and children have priority claims. We guide families through this process sensitively.
