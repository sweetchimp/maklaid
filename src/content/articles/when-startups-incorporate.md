Every founding team asks the same question at some point: can we incorporate *after* the product works? Legally you can. Financially and practically, waiting usually costs more than it saves.

## The liability clock starts immediately

The moment you sign a supplier agreement, take a customer payment or hire a contractor, someone carries the obligation personally if things go wrong. Without a company, that someone is you — jointly and without limit. Incorporation draws a line between business risk and personal assets.

## Vesting cannot be backdated properly

Investors will not touch a cap table built on verbal promises. Founder shares subject to vesting schedules need to exist from day one, documented in a shareholders' agreement. Retrofitting vesting after a dispute — or worse, after a founder walks — is expensive and often impossible.

## Tax exposure compounds quietly

Revenue collected into personal accounts creates M-Pesa statements no accountant can untangle retroactively. Corporate accounts, proper VAT registration when thresholds are crossed, and deductible expense structures only work once the legal entity exists.

## The practical answer

Incorporate when three things happen: you commit to working on the idea full-time, money changes hands in either direction, or more than one person is involved. Any one of the three justifies the modest cost of doing it right.
