Uganda's Employment Act 2006 is the primary legislation governing the employer-employee relationship. Whether you are hiring your first staff member or managing a workforce of hundreds, understanding your obligations is essential.

## Key provisions every employer must know

### Written contracts

Every employee is entitled to a written contract of employment within two weeks of starting work. The contract must specify:

- Names of employer and employee
- Job description and title
- Date of commencement
- Place of work
- Hours of work
- Remuneration and payment intervals
- Leave entitlements
- Notice periods for termination

Failure to provide a written contract does not invalidate the employment relationship — it simply means the statutory minimum terms apply.

### Working hours and overtime

The standard working week is 40 hours (8 hours per day, 5 days). Any work beyond these hours must be compensated at overtime rates:

- Weekday overtime: 1.5 times the normal hourly rate
- Weekend overtime: 2 times the normal hourly rate
- Public holiday overtime: 2 times the normal hourly rate

### Leave entitlements

- **Annual leave:** 21 working days per year (minimum)
- **Sick leave:** 2 months paid sick leave in any 12-month period (after 3 months of service)
- **Maternity leave:** 60 working days (fully paid)
- **Paternity leave:** 4 working days (fully paid)

## Recent developments

### Minimum wage

Uganda's minimum wage has been a subject of ongoing debate. The current minimum wage under the Minimum Wages Board remains at UGX 130,000 per month, though advocacy for upward revision continues.

### Occupational safety

The Occupational Safety and Health Act 2006 requires employers to:

- Provide a safe working environment
- Conduct risk assessments
- Report workplace accidents to the Ministry of Gender, Labour and Social Development
- Provide personal protective equipment where necessary

## Termination and severance

An employer may terminate employment through:

1. **Summary dismissal** — for gross misconduct (no notice required)
2. **Notice** — as specified in the contract or statutory minimum (1 month for employees with less than 1 year of service; 2 months for 1–5 years; 3 months for 5+ years)
3. **Redundancy** — requires consultation and severance pay

### Severance pay

An employee terminated through redundancy is entitled to:

- 1 week's pay for each year of service (first year)
- 2 weeks' pay for each subsequent year of service

## FAQ

**Can an employer fire without notice?**
Only in cases of gross misconduct (e.g., theft, violence, serious insubordination). Even then, the employer should follow a fair procedure and document the reasons.

**What are the consequences of not providing a written contract?**
The employee can file a complaint with the Labour Officer. The employer may face penalties and the employee is entitled to the statutory minimum terms.

**How do I handle a workplace dispute?**
Start with the internal grievance procedure. If that fails, mediation through the Labour Officer is the next step. We can represent you at any stage.
