Uganda's company registration process has been streamlined through the Uganda Registration Services Bureau (URSB). Here is what to expect at each stage.

## Step 1: Name reservation

Before incorporation, you must reserve a company name with URSB. The process:

- Submit 3 proposed names in order of preference
- URSB checks for conflicts with existing registrations
- Name is reserved for 30 days
- Fee: UGX 35,000

**Tip:** Choose names that are distinctive. Generic names like "Uganda Trading Ltd" are more likely to be rejected.

## Step 2: Prepare incorporation documents

The following documents are required:

- **Form A** — Application for registration of a company
- **Memorandum of Association** — defines the company's objects and share capital
- **Articles of Association** — governs internal management
- **Director and shareholder details** — names, addresses, nationalities, identification
- **Registered office address** — must be a physical address in Uganda

## Step 3: File with URSB

Submit the completed documents to URSB with:

- Registration fee: UGX 250,000 (private limited company)
- Stamp duty: 1% of authorised share capital (minimum UGX 25,000)
- Annual return fee: UGX 50,000

## Step 4: Receive certificate of incorporation

URSB typically processes applications within 7–14 working days. You will receive:

- Certificate of Incorporation
- Certified Memorandum and Articles of Association
- Company PIN from the Uganda Revenue Authority

## Step 5: Post-incorporation steps

After incorporation, you must:

1. **Open a company bank account** — requires the certificate, PIN, and director identification
2. **Register for VAT** — if turnover will exceed UGX 150 million per annum
3. **Register with NSSF** — for employee social security contributions
4. **Obtain a trading licence** — from the relevant local authority
5. **Sector-specific licences** — depending on your business (e.g., UCC for telecoms, BoU for financial services)

## Common mistakes

- **Using personal bank accounts** — this pierces the corporate veil and exposes directors to personal liability
- **Ignoring annual returns** — failure to file annual returns with URSB can result in the company being struck off
- **Not having a shareholders' agreement** — this is the single biggest source of corporate disputes in Uganda
- **Assuming a certificate of incorporation is enough** — you need the additional registrations listed above

## Foreign-owned companies

Foreign investors can incorporate 100% foreign-owned companies in Uganda. However:

- A minimum of one Ugandan director is recommended (not legally required but practical)
- The Uganda Investment Authority (UIA) offers incentives for registered investors
- Work permits must be obtained separately for foreign directors and employees

## FAQ

**How much does it cost to incorporate in Uganda?**
Total costs (including legal fees, stamp duty and government fees) typically range from UGX 1–3 million, depending on authorised share capital and complexity.

**Can I register online?**
URSB offers an online registration portal at ursb.go.ug. However, we recommend legal review of all documents before filing.

**How long does the process take?**
Typically 7–14 working days from filing to certificate of incorporation. Post-incorporation registrations add 2–4 weeks.
