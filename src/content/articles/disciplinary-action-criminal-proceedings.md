**Yes.** An employer is generally not required to wait for the conclusion of criminal proceedings before commencing disciplinary action, unless the employment contract or applicable law provides otherwise.

## Legal Position

Under Section 94 of the Employment Act, Cap. 226, disciplinary proceedings may proceed independently of criminal prosecution. The courts have similarly recognised that employment disciplinary proceedings and criminal proceedings are separate processes.

## Key Considerations for Employers

When facing an employee involved in criminal proceedings, employers have two main options:

1. **Proceed with disciplinary action** while the criminal case is ongoing; or
2. **Defer disciplinary action** until the criminal proceedings are concluded.

### Procedural Fairness Requirements

Where disciplinary action is taken, procedural fairness must be observed. The employee should:

1. Be informed of the allegations
2. Be given an opportunity to respond
3. Have access to the material facts necessary to make a meaningful defence
4. Be given an opportunity to make representations on the proposed sanction

## Key Takeaway

A pending criminal case does not automatically prevent an employer from taking disciplinary action. Employers may act on reasonably available evidence without waiting indefinitely for the criminal justice process, provided that the disciplinary process is fair and lawful.
