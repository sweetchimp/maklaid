Kenya's Employment (Amendment) Act 2026 introduces the most significant changes to the employment relationship in a decade. Employers who treat it as a paperwork exercise will feel the consequences in court; those who adapt now will find the new rules manageable.

## What changed for contracts

The amendments require written particulars of employment to be issued within **seven days** of commencement — down from two months. Every contract must now state the place of work, hours, and any probation terms expressly. Contracts that predate the amendment must be reissued to existing staff by the compliance deadline in March 2027.

## Termination gets stricter

Three changes matter most:

1. Notice periods for employees with over five years' service have increased.
2. Employers must document a genuine performance-improvement process before summary dismissal on capability grounds.
3. The burden of proving fair procedure now sits squarely on the employer, with tribunals directed to award compensation where records are missing.

## What employers should do now

Audit every template contract against the new requirements. Reissue existing contracts early rather than waiting for the deadline. And train line managers: most unfair-termination awards trace back to a conversation that should never have happened the way it did.
